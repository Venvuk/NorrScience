How to Spot the 4 NOVA Food Groups

Developed by researchers at the University of São Paulo led by Carlos Monteiro, the NOVA classification sorts foods not by calories or nutrients, but by the nature, extent, and purpose of industrial processing.

Open Food Facts NOVA ↗ FAO NOVA Report ↗
NOVA GROUP 1

Unprocessed or Minimally Processed Foods

Edible parts of plants or animals in their natural state, or altered only by drying, crushing, grinding, pasteurizing, chilling, freezing, or plain fermentation.

Label Detective Rule:

Usually a single whole ingredient (or live cultures in plain yogurt). Zero added salt, sugar, oils, fats, or cosmetic additives.

NOVA GROUP 2

Processed Culinary Ingredients

Substances obtained directly from Group 1 foods or from nature by pressing, refining, grinding, milling, or drying to prepare, season, and cook Group 1 meals.

Label Detective Rule:

Includes olive & seed oils, unsalted butter, sea salt, cane sugar, pure honey, and culinary vinegars.

NOVA GROUP 3

Processed Foods

Manufactured by adding Group 2 culinary ingredients (salt, sugar, oil, vinegar) to Group 1 whole foods via canning, bottling, curing, fermentation, or baking.

Label Detective Rule:

Formula = Group 1 Food + Group 2 Ingredient (2–4 kitchen-recognizable ingredients, zero emulsifiers or artificial flavors).

NOVA GROUP 4

Ultra-Processed Food & Drink Products (UPFs)

Industrial formulations made from extracted food substances (protein isolates, modified starches, hydrogenated oils, HFCS) and cosmetic additives.

Label Detective Rule:

Spot at least one non-kitchen marker: emulsifiers (soy lecithin, mono-/diglycerides, DATEM), artificial flavors, synthetic dyes, MSG, or sucralose.
